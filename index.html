The problem

Briefs don't fail all at once. Meaning slips at every handover.

  1. What Clara imagines

    Her grandmother's guesthouse, morning light on the harbour.

  2. What she says

    “Warm, but not rustic.”

  3. What gets heard

    “Warm.”

  4. What gets made

    Driftwood textures and a rope monogram.

  5. What she reacts to

    “This isn't what I meant.”

By the time a client says it, the original intent is buried in emails, calls and file names. Mysa keeps it in view, next to the work, for the whole project.

How Mysa works

One thread runs from the brief to the approved work.

Each step keeps a reference to the one before it, so anyone can see why the work looks the way it does.

  1. 01 · Direction

    Agree what the words mean

    Turn the client's adjectives into shared phrases you can both point to later.

    “Coastal in light and colour, never anchors and rope.”

    Agreed

  2. 02 · Work

    Show the work as work

    Versions are presented large, never as a row of file names.

    Fenwick House Version 02

  3. 03 · Feedback

    Pin feedback to the thing

    A note belongs to a version and an area, not a general thread.

    2 Wordmark · V02

    “The name feels a little tight, like it's holding its breath.”

  4. 04 · Decision

    Record what you agreed

    What changes, what stays and why. Both of you agree before work moves on.

    Decision 02

    Give the wordmark more air.

    Changes: letter spacing · Stays: serif, palette

  5. 05 · Approval

    Approve the work itself

    Agreeing on a decision is not approving a version. Mysa keeps them apart.
